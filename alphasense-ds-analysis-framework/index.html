Experiment Design Portfolio

The B2B Experiment Constraint

You cannot do a clean holdout of a core feature on enterprise B2B users the way a consumer app can. Annual contracts, procurement cycles, and enterprise expectations require creative causal identification.

Staggered Rollout DiDAny new GenAI feature launch with phased rolloutsuccess PrimaryRequires parallel trends assumption. Firm-size differences across waves can confound
Within-Firm HoldoutFeature launches where individual randomization is possiblesuccess PrimarySUTVA risk — same-firm users compare notes; ethical tension with holdout group

Strategic Scenarios

Scenario A: GenAI IS driving habit Scenario A

Signals: Scheduled agents growing, unprompted return rising, D30 retention gap >8pp between adopters and non-adopters

Strategic Actions

  • Reframe pricing: AI-tier or usage-based pricing becomes defensible
  • Collapse the activation funnel: bottleneck is getting users to first high-quality output
  • Invest in scheduled agents as #1 habit anchor — push-to-pull is your retention moat
  • Personalization: use query history to suggest next research
  • Mobile as second habit trigger: complete the loop proactively
Moat

Institutional knowledge lock-in: saved agents, grids, templates, and query history make switching catastrophically expensive

Scenario B: GenAI is NOT driving habit Scenario B

Signals: D30 retention gap between GenAI users and non-users narrows or disappears, novelty decay visible

Strategic Actions

  • Stop optimizing GenAI engagement volume — it doesn't predict retention
  • Diagnose which specific features do show retention signal
  • Invest in onboarding: the problem is likely activation, not the feature itself
  • Qualitative research: interview 20 churned users who tried GenAI
  • Re-examine the pricing model: don't charge for features that don't retain
Moat

Traditional search moat remains; focus on workflow integration and data coverage depth
